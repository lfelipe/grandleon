# Places along the road to Twin Gate

These five battles should feel like neighbouring places in one border region.
The road leaves cultivated farmland, crosses Cedar forest, climbs to a ruined
signal fort, enters a working village, and ends at the keep above it.

Use warm fieldstone, weathered oak, muted plaster and clay roofs throughout.
Building size, road width and tree scale should remain consistent between maps.
The March and Cedar armies bring their own banners and equipment; their presence
does not turn the village into a random mix of architectural styles.

| Phase | The place | What makes it look and play differently |
|---|---|---|
| Bell Orchard | A working orchard beside a farm road. | Orderly tree rows, grass headlands and a small bell court. Keep the tutorial compact and readable. Rowan follows the upper road; Akari enters along the lower orchard track. Farm buildings can sit outside the playable board. |
| Cedar Deepwood | An old forest road following a brook. | Dense, connected woodland surrounds two irregular clearings. Fallen timber and roots belong at woodland edges; large old trees anchor the bends. Solo starts beyond the quiet approach. Co-op companies clear separate trails before meeting at the road out. No isolated stone tower. |
| Cedar Watch | A ruined hill fort that once guarded the road. | A signal platform belongs inside broken defensive walls, with a supply court and visible approaches below it. Rubble marks collapsed walls, rather than making a decorative ring. Solo defends the inner court; co-op divides the western road and eastern steps during the survival battle. |
| Gate Village | A small market settlement at the keep road. | Homes and workshops face a main street. The well anchors a paved square; a quieter lane serves cottages and their gardens. The cart belongs in the loading yard. A tower joins the town wall at the eastern gate. Co-op enters by the main street and southern lane, then converges in the square. |
| Twin Gate Keep | The fortified seat above the village. | The approach leads through a service court to a connected gatehouse and curtain wall. Towers reinforce corners and gates. The keep sits on a grounded stone terrace against the ridge; entering reveals its chamber. Solo reaches a nearby defended approach; co-op separates the upper road and lower court before the final room. |

## Placement rules

- Start with landform, road connections and building plots. Add details after
  the full map reads as a place.
- Give each district a dominant use. Fields belong outside dense housing;
  workshops need access to the loading yard; walls must connect to something.
- Put the entrance and exit on believable continuations of the same road.
  Use landmarks to explain where to go without covering units or movement marks.
- Keep open spaces intentional: a market square, a clearing, a courtyard or a
  defensive approach. Avoid alternating terrain types just to add colour.
- Vary a coherent family: related roofs and stonework, several tree ages,
  patches of orchard fruit, weathered and repaired wall sections. Do not use
  unrelated styles as a substitute for variety.
- Review each map at gameplay zoom and as a full overview, in both player
  modes. Check approach distances, flanking routes, covered units and retreats.

## Movement in the scenery

Use a shared, slow wind direction for leaves, grass, hanging cloth and loose
branches, with small differences in timing. Water can ripple and the watch's
signal fire can flicker. Stone walls, carts and buildings stay still. Motion
must stay subtle enough that movement ranges and unit actions remain clear.

## Build order

1. **Gate Village:** establish the street, market, cottage gardens and connected
   gate wall. This is the first layout prototype; judge the whole settlement,
   then refine materials and small details.
2. **Console versions:** export the same landmark shapes to both engines,
   simplify detail deliberately and compare the same village views. Do not
   substitute generic mountain scenery for the buildings.
3. **Bell Orchard and Deepwood:** make cultivated rows and natural woodland
   visibly different; give each route a clear origin and destination.
4. **Cedar Watch and Twin Gate:** replace decorative paved/rubble patterns with
   connected fortifications, service spaces and a convincing approach.

Gate Village now has the first revised layout, with initial models in all three
renderers. The other four rows describe planned composition work, not finished
maps. Console prop animation and PS1 village performance still need work. Current automated village traces
finish in 13 solo rounds and 6 co-op rounds; the earlier solo pacing/attrition
baseline flags this change for further playtesting. Do not force casualties or
change combat rules to satisfy an old trace.
