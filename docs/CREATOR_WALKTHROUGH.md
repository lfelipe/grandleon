# The Last Ferry

Read the quoted parts as the author’s brief. Try each task before looking at
**Where to look**. This is a script for reviewing Grandleon’s editor with one
author, using an original five-battle adventure that grows from a single map.
Start in an empty project library and use **New Campaign** with its built-in
starter. Keep the same project through export.

## Get one battle playing

1. **Name the game and its heroes.** “I want to make a short adventure about
   running the last ferry upriver. Iona, the ferrymaster, uses a sword. Fen
   keeps the crew alive. Losing Iona should lose the battle.”

   **Where to look** · **Battle Guide → Edit Heroes**, then the hero’s role,
   kit and defeat choices. Try an incompatible starting weapon once.
   **Check** · The form names the conflict in place and accepts the correction.
   After save and reopen, the two names, effective equipment and defeat rules
   still match. A wounded ally can receive useful healing in play.

2. **Shape the Ferry Landing.** “I want one short path over paving and a longer
   route through trees. Put a house beside the landing and a bell over two
   tiles. I want the paths to be easy to read.”

   **Where to look** · **Edit Terrain and Rooms → Terrain / Landmarks**. Use an
   8×6 map, preview the bell, and try placing it at the right edge. Remove it
   from its second tile, then undo. **Check** · Placement and removal affect
   the whole footprint and the edit is reversible. The bell draws in the
   player, and the two routes are passable.

3. **Fight, edit and fight again.** “Put Iona and Fen at the landing and place
   two ordinary guards. I should win after both guards fall. I want to try it
   before making anything else.”

   **Where to look** · **Place Heroes and Extras → Edit Objective → Test 1P**
   on the Battle Guide. **Check** · The author can place both guards from one
   archetype. Players get a legal attack by round 2, the first guard’s defeat
   leaves the battle running, and the second ends it. In a separate attempt,
   losing Iona ends the battle. Return to the same battle, move one guard a
   square, and verify the new location in the next test. Check the authored
   loss route during the full adventure run.

4. **Give the second player a crew.** “I want 1P to start nearer the landing.
   In 2P, Sela brings a second crew from the other route, and an extra guard
   threatens that approach.”

   **Where to look** · **Campaign Outline → Players** for the launch choice,
   then the **1P / 2P** tabs in **Map & Battle** for formations. Create and
   place Sela, then use **Player Ownership** in **Battle Setup** or **Assign
   Player 2** beside a blocked 2P test. Use **2P-Only Units** for Sela’s crew
   and the extra guard, and **Two-Player Opening Story** for her introduction.
   **Check** · The ownership shortcut opens the correct 2P formation. The 1P
   start stays put as the 2P start moves. Only 2P gets the extra guard and the
   extra opening line. Each player makes a useful decision with their own crew.

## Grow it into a campaign

5. **Carry earnings into Reed Road.** “I want one guard to leave a blade and
   enough gold for a tonic. I want a shop before the next battle with one
   tonic for three gold.”

   **Where to look** · **Pack & Drops** on the guard’s archetype, then **Add
   Next Battle** and **Shop before battle** on Reed Road. Give the drop to a
   separate archetype if only one guard should have it. **Check** · Trade a
   carried item for the blade when the pack is full. The other hero’s pack
   stays the same. Play the adventure from its opening to earn the gold, buy
   the tonic and verify the shared balance, stock, buyer and both 2P Ready
   choices. Resume at the next battle and check the purchase persists.

6. **Hold the Pump House.** “I want to survive 20 rounds and keep Iona alive.
   Enemies arrive from the west in round 3 and from the east in rounds 6 and
   9. In 2P another group arrives from the south in round 5.”

   **Where to look** · **Edit Objective → This Battle**, then **Battle Setup →
   Opening Forces & Waves**. Type the round numbers and check the schedule
   before applying it. Use **Edit Goal** to turn the earlier defeat-all
   rule into a survival goal. Adjust its deadline after a playtest.
   **Check** ·
   Each wave enters from the authored side on its round, and the extra wave
   appears only in 2P. Clearing the first wave leaves the battle active.
   Surviving round 20 takes the victory route. Losing Iona takes the loss
   route in a separate attempt.

7. **Make Flood Market about a named opponent.** “I want players to decide
   whether to go down the main street or around the houses. The foreman is
   the target. Give Iona a short opening line, and give the foreman a line
   when the battle turns.”

   **Where to look** · **Edit Terrain and Rooms**, **Edit Objective**, **Write
   Story**, and **Battle Setup → Scripted Battle Moments** for the trigger.
   **Check** · The goal points to the correct placed opponent. Defeating an
   ordinary guard leaves the battle active. Defeating the named target still
   wins with a guard left. The selected scene plays once at its intended
   moment, and the victory route leads to Sluice Hall.

8. **Reveal Sluice Hall and finish.** “Put the final opponent inside the keep.
   I want the roof to open up as a unit goes through the doorway. Then end on
   a short scene about getting the ferry through.”

   **Where to look** · Castle terrain and **Interior Visibility** on the map,
   then the **Campaign Outline** for the ending route. If the previous
   battle has both victory and defeat routes, use **Continue On** in **Add
   Next Battle** to extend the victory route. **Check** · The interior
   starts covered. Moving the cursor alone leaves it covered. Crossing
   the doorway reveals the intended interior and retreat leaves it revealed.
   Win the last battle and see the ending once. Run **Play Adventure 1P**
   and **Play Adventure 2P** from the opening to check the entire sequence.

9. **Translate, back up and share.** “I want Brazilian Portuguese players to
   see the goal, one speaker’s name and the last line in their language. I
   want to keep an editable copy and give someone else a playable one.”

   **Where to look** · **Translations**, then **Export → Finish & Export**.
   **Check** · Edits survive changing the translation selection. The player
   shows the three translations and falls back to the source for another
   line. Reopen the editable archive in a fresh library, change a line and
   play it. Open the playable export independently and see the original line.

## How to use the results

For each numbered task, record where the author first looked, which control
they used, any help or detour, and what changed in play. Check desktop keyboard
and mouse and a narrow touch layout. Open the same step in Brazilian
Portuguese to check labels and recovery messages. A step passes when the
author finds it, can correct an invalid edit where it happens, and sees the
intended result after save, reopen or play. A passing automated check confirms
the behavior it exercises. Discoverability requires observing a person using
the author’s brief.

The guide now puts **Assign Player 2** beside a blocked battle test, and
**Battle Setup** exposes **2P-Only Units**. The automated guide check follows
those controls into the correct map, assigns a real ally, and opens both
player modes to check which crew appears. Track the full five-battle run and
the next usability changes in the [0.6 checklist](../TODO.md#first-battle-and-named-characters).

## Lessons from the references

- **Fire Emblem** shows how preparation, threat and terrain shape the opening
  moves. The authoring check asks whether players can read the opening routes,
  identify the first threat, and recognize when an optional preparation step
  matters. [Fire Emblem Heroes formation walkthrough](https://new-guide.fire-emblem-heroes.com/en-GB/feh-1110.html), [Shadow Dragon manual](https://www.nintendo.com/eu/media/downloads/games_8/emanuals/nintendo_ds_21/Manual_NintendoDS_FireEmblemShadowDragon_EN.pdf)
- **Final Fantasy Tactics** illustrates roles with different useful actions,
  terrain and turn order. The check asks whether the author can tell what each
  hero contributes and see it in an early test. [Developer tactics and role advice](https://blog.playstation.com/2025/07/21/the-secrets-behind-final-fantasy-tactics-the-ivalice-chronicles-makeover/), [Square Enix combat overview](https://na.store.square-enix-games.com/final-fantasy-tactics---the-ivalice-chronicles)
- **Disgaea** makes tile effects visible and shows what changes when a player
  interacts with the board. The check uses the keep reveal and the consequences
  of collecting loot. Authored tile effects and more map interactions are ideas
  for later review. [NIS America’s Geo Effects walkthrough](https://www.nisamerica.com/disgaea/dis_d2/system/geo_effect.html)
