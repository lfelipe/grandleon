# PC character prototypes

`rook_commander_hd.png` is the first higher-resolution sprite replacement used
to exercise the PC graphics-profile contract with production-shaped input. It
is a transparent 4×2 sheet with 443×443 cells in row-major order:

1. idle A;
2. idle B;
3. walk A;
4. walk B;
5. weapon wind-up;
6. weapon follow-through;
7. relay cast;
8. hurt or defeat.

The image was generated specifically for this prototype, then trimmed to exact
cell boundaries. The runtime consumes this committed PNG directly; screenshots
of it in play are therefore implementation evidence rather than concept art.
Its binding, pivot, and timing live in `content/graphics_profiles.json`. Removing
that one binding or asset restores the generated 32-pixel commander without a
content, package, or rules change.

`relay_commander.glb` is the first imported, skinned 3D character. It contains a
sixteen-bone humanoid skeleton, seven material surfaces, and six in-place clips
exported with deliberately non-semantic DCC names. The full-voxel profile maps
those names explicitly, proving that a replacement art pack does not need to
adopt the engine's spelling. Its ceramic armour, helmet and visor, relay core,
shield, sword, and cape are readable at the real board scale.

The GLB is generated by `platform/godot/tools/export_relay_commander.py` through
Blender. Run `platform/godot/scripts/generate-imported-characters.sh` to
reproduce it. The checked-in binary is the player input; Blender is needed only
to revise the source asset and is not shipped in a PC bundle.

`relay_commander_3d.tscn` remains the earlier procedural reference.
`relay_drone_3d.tscn` is still the authored beast replacement: its shared script
constructs one-cell-scale geometry and installs in-place `idle`, `run`,
`attack`, `ability`, `hurt`, and `defeat` clips. Every unbound archetype keeps
the procedural voxel fallback. The next asset-pipeline increment is an imported
drone GLB followed by a second character using the humanoid skeleton.
